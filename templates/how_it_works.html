System Pipeline

How the system works

Seven stages take a submitted story from raw text to a re-verifiable record — four belong to the AI model, three belong to the traceability ledger.

{{ section_heading("The seven stages", kicker="Submission to verification") }}
  1. 01 {{ icon('doc', 20) }}

    News submission

    A headline or article body is pasted into the analysis workspace.

  2. 02 {{ icon('search', 20) }}

    Preprocessing

    HTML, links and noise are stripped; the text is lower-cased and normalised.

  3. 03 {{ icon('spark', 20) }}

    Feature extraction

    TF-IDF 1–2 grams plus eight writing-style statistics form the feature vector.

  4. 04 {{ icon('scale', 20) }}

    AI classification

    Logistic Regression returns a FAKE / REAL label with a probability for each.

  5. 05 {{ icon('hash', 20) }}

    Content fingerprint

    SHA-256 is computed over the article text as its permanent fingerprint.

  6. 06 {{ icon('nodes', 20) }}

    Ledger entry

    The record is appended to the hash chain, linked by its previous entry hash.

  7. 07 {{ icon('shield', 20) }}

    Verification

    Anyone re-runs the fingerprint check and walks the chain back to genesis.

{{ section_heading("Two different questions", kicker="AI versus blockchain") }}

{{ icon('spark', 20) }} The AI model

Answers: “Does this text read like the labelled examples it was trained on?”

  • {{ icon('check', 16) }}Trained on 1,991 curated ISOT news articles.
  • {{ icon('check', 16) }}Outputs a probability, not a certainty.
  • {{ icon('x', 16) }}Cannot confirm whether a claim is factually true.
Predicts the writing pattern — never the truth.

{{ icon('chain', 20) }} The traceability ledger

Answers: “Has this stored record been altered since it was written?”

  • {{ icon('check', 16) }}SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact submitted text.
  • {{ icon('check', 16) }}Hash chain links every entry back to genesis.
  • {{ icon('x', 16) }}Does not — and cannot — judge the article's content.
Proves the record is unchanged — not that the story is true.

{{ icon('scale', 16) }} Why both? A verdict that cannot be audited is untrustworthy, and a record that cannot be re-checked proves nothing. The model produces the verdict; the ledger makes the verdict accountable.
